Postdoctoral Researcher · Heavy-Ion Physics

Chasing the quark–gluon plasma
one collision at a time.

Theoretical Physics Department — CERN

I study the QCD phase diagram through relativistic heavy-ion collisions — how bulk nuclear matter melts into a deconfined plasma of quarks and gluons, and what its flow, fluctuations and vorticity tell us about the strong force. Currently based at Geneva, Switzerland.

Research

What I work on

Three threads run through my current research program.

Multi-fluid Hydrodynamics with Continuous Freeze-out

Anisotropic flow & QGP transport properties

Extracting the shear and bulk viscosity of the quark-gluon plasma from flow-harmonic measurements in Pb–Pb and small collision systems.

Equation of State of Dense Nuclear Matter

Initial-state fluctuations

Modeling event-by-event fluctuations in the nuclear overlap region and their propagation into final-state observables.

Phase Diagram and QCD Critical End Point

Search for the QCD critical point

Using beam-energy-scan data and fluctuation observables to map the conjectured critical point in the QCD phase diagram.

The phase diagram

Where this research sits

Nuclear matter changes character as temperature and net-baryon density rise — from ordinary hadronic matter, through a crossover, toward a deconfined quark–gluon plasma. A conjectured critical point may separate a smooth crossover from a first-order transition. My work sits at the beam-energy-scan region, probing exactly this structure.
