FRISCO3

FRISCO3 — Multi-Fluid Hydrodynamics with Continuous Decoupling

Active [2023] – present

A relativistic multi-fluid hydrodynamic model with a continuous decoupling of hadrons from the fluid.

hydrodynamics heavy-ion collisions freeze-out

The model

FRISCO3 (3-Fluid Relativistic heavy Ion collision Simulation with Continuous decOupling) is a relativistic multi-fluid hydrodynamic model with continuous hadron decoupling. The model disentangles the hydrodynamic equations of motion of the whole system into multiple fluids. A useful choice is to treat the two incoming nuclei as two distinct nuclear fluids, which dynamically generate the equilibrated fireball, the third fluid, via friction terms.

The fluid-fluid friction terms are derived from the measured inelastic nucleon-nucleon cross section for energy- and longitudinal-momentum loss, as well as baryon-number transport into the fireball — i.e. stopping.

The continuous decoupling is a generalization of the commonly applied Cooper–Frye method and overcomes its caveats. The local decoupling rate of a hadron is determined from the local emissivity, given by the scattering rate, the in-medium width, and the expansion rate, and the survival probability, or optical depth, along the hadron's trajectory to the free-streaming regime.

The model is thus ideally suited for studying compressed baryonic matter at the upcoming FAIR facility.
